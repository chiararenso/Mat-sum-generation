## Abstract

Sharing human-mobility data is scientifically valuable but legally and ethically constrained:
raw trajectories are hyper-identifying, and data owners are reluctant to release them. Synthetic
data is the standard escape, yet existing trajectory generators are *geometry-centric* — they
reproduce where people go but discard the rich semantics (activities, points of interest, transport
modes) that make mobility data useful, and the most faithful (deep) generators tend to memorize
real traces. We propose to generate synthetic mobility in a **semantic state space** rather than a
raw geometric one, obtained from **MAT-SUM**, a location-centric summarization method that abstracts
a city into *semantic locations*. On this substrate we build a compact pipeline: an anonymized
semantic vocabulary with a tunable generalization/anonymity dial, a mechanistic (Markov) sequence
generator with an empirical dwell-time model, an optional deep (LSTM) alternative, and a
spatially-coupled instantiation step that re-materializes trajectories over real geography without
copying any real path. We evaluate on a small private sample (Akkodis, Paris) as a proof-of-concept
and, at scale, on a public dataset of 581 de-identified individuals in Paris. The semantic
abstraction yields three properties that no single existing technique offers together: (i)
*semantic richness* — the output is a Multiple-Aspect Trajectory, not a coordinate stream; (ii)
*controllable, structural privacy* — a single interpretable dial spans the fidelity/privacy frontier
and the mechanistic generator produces **zero verbatim copies** at every setting, with a
distance-to-closest-record at or above the real-to-real baseline; and (iii) *data efficiency* —
fidelity improves monotonically with the number of individuals while a comparable deep model
"wins" only by memorizing at small sample sizes and loses that lead at scale. We argue this makes
location-centric semantic summarization a natural foundation for privacy-preserving mobility-data
sharing.

## 1. Introduction

Movement data generated by GPS-enabled devices underpins research in urban planning, epidemiology,
transportation and behavioral modeling. Yet the very properties that make such data useful also make
it dangerous to release: individual traces are remarkably unique — a handful of spatio-temporal
points suffices to re-identify most people [de Montjoye et al. 2013] — so data owners (telecom
operators, mobility companies, municipalities) keep it locked away. This produces a chronic
bottleneck: the organizations holding the data cannot share it, and researchers who could extract
value from it cannot obtain it.

**Synthetic data** is the widely-adopted resolution: replace real records with artificial ones that
preserve aggregate structure while breaking the link to real individuals. For trajectories, a rich
literature exists — mechanistic models grounded in statistical laws of human mobility (EPR
[Song et al. 2010], DITRAS/d-EPR [Pappalardo & Simini 2018], TimeGeo [Jiang et al. 2016]),
deep generative models (MoveSim [Feng et al. 2020], LSTM-TrajGAN [Rao et al. 2020], DiffTraj
[Zhu et al. 2023]), and differentially-private synthesizers (AdaTrace [Gürsoy et al. 2018],
DP-Star). Two gaps persist. First, almost all of these operate on **geometry** — they generate
coordinates or grid cells, treating semantics (what a place *is*, what activity happens there) as
absent or as a single side attribute. Second, the most faithful generators are prone to
**memorization**, silently reproducing real traces and defeating the privacy goal.

Our starting observation is that a recent summarization method, **MAT-SUM** [Pugliese et al. 2023],
already provides the missing representation. MAT-SUM enriches a geographic area with semantic aspects
(from OpenStreetMap) and merges cells that share a semantic context into *semantic locations*,
turning each trajectory into a weighted sequence over a finite semantic vocabulary. We make the case
that this is not merely a compression device but a **generative substrate**: generation performed
over semantic locations inherits everything *above the abstraction line* — semantics, coarse space,
coarse time — while resampling everything below it — exact geometry and timing. This is precisely
what buys both **scale** (the model reasons over semantic *types*, so it can instantiate new,
non-copied trajectories) and **privacy** (mapping a raw point to a generalized semantic location is
a form of semantic spatial cloaking).

The motivation is concrete and practical. A data owner such as a mobility company will not hand over
raw data on faith; but a small, non-confidential sample can be used to demonstrate that a semantic,
non-memorizing generator produces useful yet safe output, unlocking controlled access to the full
dataset from which a distributable synthetic version is then generated. This paper takes the first
two steps of that argument: it establishes the method and validates its key properties, first on a
small private sample and then on a public dataset at scale.

**Contributions.**
1. A pipeline that turns MAT-SUM's semantic locations into a **privacy-preserving generative model**
   of Multiple-Aspect Trajectories, with an interpretable generalization/anonymity dial.
2. A **spatially-coupled** instantiation step that re-materializes semantic sequences over real
   geography, recovering realistic locality without copying real paths.
3. An empirical study — proof-of-concept on a private sample and validation on 581 public
   individuals — showing the semantic abstraction delivers semantic richness, controllable privacy
   and data efficiency, and that a simple mechanistic generator dominates a deep one on the
   fidelity/privacy trade-off.

## 2. Background and Related Work

**Trajectory summarization and semantic trajectories.** Semantic (Multiple-Aspect) trajectories
enrich raw movement with heterogeneous aspects [Spaccapietra et al. 2008; Bogorny et al. — MASTER
2019]. MAT-SUM [Pugliese et al. 2023] adopts a *location-centric* view: it tessellates an area,
enriches tiles with OSM aspects (POIs, land use, public transport), merges tiles with identical
semantic context into semantic locations, and summarizes each trajectory as a weighted sequence over
them, quantified by a summarization rate (`S_rate`) and semantic quality (MUITAS [Petry et al. 2019]).
We reuse MAT-SUM as-is for the map-enrichment step and repurpose its output for generation.

**Mobility generation.** Mechanistic models reproduce empirical laws (visitation frequency, radius of
gyration, jump-length, waiting time) via exploration/preferential-return dynamics [Song et al. 2010;
Pappalardo & Simini 2018]; they are interpretable and data-efficient. Deep generators
[Feng et al. 2020; Rao et al. 2020; Zhu et al. 2023] achieve higher geometric fidelity given large
corpora. Differentially-private synthesizers privatize a mobility model (e.g., noisy Markov counts)
before sampling [Gürsoy et al. 2018]. To our knowledge, generation of *semantically rich* MATs — and
the use of a semantic summarization as the generative substrate — is largely unexplored; this is the
gap we target.

**Evaluation.** We adopt the fidelity/utility/privacy triad, with utility measured by
train-on-synthetic/test-on-real (TSTR) and privacy by membership inference, distance-to-closest-record
(DCR), and copy/uniqueness analysis.

## 3. Research Questions

- **RQ1 (semantic richness).** Does generating over semantic locations produce Multiple-Aspect
  trajectories whose *semantics* are preserved, in a way geometry-centric generators cannot?
- **RQ2 (controllable privacy).** Does the semantic generalization/anonymity dial (τ, k) provide a
  controllable fidelity↔privacy frontier, and does the generator avoid memorizing real individuals?
- **RQ3 (data efficiency).** How does synthesis quality behave as the number of individuals grows,
  and how does a mechanistic generator compare to a deep one across data regimes — especially the
  small-sample regime typical of restricted-access settings?
- **RQ4 (spatial realism & transfer).** Can semantic sequences be re-materialized into
  spatially-realistic trajectories over real geography, and does the semantic abstraction enable
  placement beyond the training region? `[cross-city transfer: pending NYC run]`

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Overview: the abstraction line

Let a dataset of raw trajectories over a geographic area be given. MAT-SUM maps it to (a) a set of
**semantic locations** and (b) for each trajectory, an ordered, temporally-weighted sequence of the
semantic locations it visits. Our generator operates on (b) over the vocabulary (a). Conceptually,
everything *above the abstraction line* — the semantic identity of places and the coarse
spatio-temporal structure — is modeled and preserved; everything *below it* — the exact coordinates
and timestamps — is discarded and later resampled. Preserved properties are therefore exactly those
MAT-SUM retains, which its own `S_rate`/MUITAS analysis quantifies; freed degrees of freedom are what
enable both scaling and privacy.

### 4.2 Anonymized semantic vocabulary (Phase 0)

From the semantic-mapped trajectories we form ordered **visit sequences** by run-length-collapsing
consecutive points that fall in the same semantic context, accumulating their dwell time. Each
semantic location is represented by a **symbol**: the set of its top-*m* TF-IDF semantic aspects.
The granularity *m* controls the vocabulary size (few, coarse symbols for small *m*; many, fine
symbols for large *m*). We then enforce **k-anonymity on the vocabulary**: any symbol occurring fewer
than *k* times is merged into the most Jaccard-similar frequent symbol, guaranteeing that every
symbol in the alphabet is supported by at least *k* occurrences and eliminating singleton,
identifying contexts. The pair (*m*, *k*) is the method's **privacy/utility dial** — the direct
analogue of MAT-SUM's similarity threshold τ — governing semantic resolution, and hence jointly
governing compression, semantic quality and privacy.

### 4.3 Sequence generators (Phase 1)

**Mechanistic (Markov).** We fit a first-order Markov chain over the symbol alphabet with Laplace
smoothing, together with an empirical per-symbol dwell-time distribution. Trajectory length is drawn
from the empirical length distribution (a DITRAS-style decoupling of *how many* from *where*), which
reproduces realistic lengths without a termination state whose probability would be diluted over a
large alphabet. Sampling yields new symbol sequences with dwell times. Transition and dwell counts
can be privatized with calibrated noise for a formal (ε,δ)-DP guarantee. `[formal DP: pending]`

**Deep (LSTM).** As a learned alternative we train a small LSTM language model over the same alphabet
and the same dwell model, isolating the sequence model. This lets us test whether a higher-capacity
generator earns its fidelity honestly or by memorizing training individuals.

### 4.4 Spatial instantiation (Phases 2–3)

To obtain trajectories over real geography, each generated symbol is realized at a concrete enriched
tile of that semantic type.

**Decoupled (Phase 2).** Independently for each visit, choose a tile of the required symbol with an
exploration/preferential-return gravity weighting. This preserves the coarse spatial spread but,
because the semantic sequence is spatially blind, breaks micro-locality: the nearest tile of a given
fine semantic type may be far, inflating jump lengths.

**Spatially-coupled (Phase 3).** We instead generate directly at the tile level with a transition
that couples three factors — learned semantic compatibility `T[s_cur, s_next]`, spatial proximity
`exp(-d/scale)`, and a visitation prior — wrapped in an EPR explore/return process [Song et al. 2010].
Locality then *emerges* from the process rather than being imposed. Optionally, consecutive chosen
locations are connected with road-network routing (OSRM) to produce road-consistent, plausible-but-
invented GPS geometry; because tiles are chosen anew, no real path is reproduced.

### 4.5 Privacy as controllable semantic generalization

Mapping raw points to generalized semantic locations is a semantic form of the spatial-cloaking /
k-anonymity primitive of location privacy [Gruteser & Grunwald 2003], but generalizing by *meaning*
rather than by a blind grid, so it preserves utility. The (*m*, *k*) dial sets the generalization
strength; generation of *new* sequences (rather than resampling real ones) addresses the
sequence-level uniqueness threat; and the spatial-instantiation and routing steps re-introduce
realism without re-introducing the original data.

### 4.6 Evaluation metrics

*Fidelity*: distributional distances (total variation / Jensen–Shannon) between real and synthetic on
semantic unigram/bigram distributions and MUITAS; and spatial radius of gyration and jump-length.
*Utility*: TSTR on a semantic downstream task `[pending]`. *Privacy*: exact- and near-copy rate
(MUITAS ≥ 0.95) against the training set; distance-to-closest-record (DCR = 1 − nearest-neighbor
MUITAS) versus the real-to-real baseline; membership inference `[pending at scale]`.

## 5. Experimental Validation

### 5.1 Datasets

**Akkodis / Paris (proof-of-concept, private).** A small confidential sample of 20 GPS trajectories
in Paris (4,039 points). Used only to establish the pipeline; not a basis for quantitative claims.

**Paris-581 (validation, public).** The Paris subset of the semantically-enriched OSM mobility dataset
of [Pugliese et al. 2025] (Zenodo 15658129): raw GPS traces merged **one trajectory per de-identified
OpenStreetMap contributor**, i.e. **581 individuals**, 1.57 M points. Because merging is per user, the
privacy unit is the person and per-trajectory metrics are per-person. (A spatial home-clustering
heuristic gave a fragile 260–478 estimate contradicting this authoritative count and was discarded;
the residual multi-account confound is rare and noted as a limitation.) Timestamps are real
(2007–2024); only user ids are de-identified. We ran MAT-SUM map-enrichment on this area (squares,
zoom 17): 32,697 enriched tiles → 18,554 semantic locations. `[New York (18,765 individuals): pending]`

### 5.2 Summarization at scale (context for RQ1)

On Paris-581, MAT-SUM attains a dataset summarization rate **S_rate = 0.921** (per-individual range
0.45–0.996), within the published Geolife range (0.90–0.98). The proof-of-concept's lower 0.738 was
not a method weakness but data sparsity — the PoC trajectories are short (84–481 points) while the
validation trajectories are densely sampled — confirming that dense semantic trajectories summarize,
and hence compress into few semantic locations, very effectively.

### 5.3 Granularity, fidelity and privacy (RQ2)

Table 1 sweeps the (*m*, *k*) dial on Paris-581 (N=200 synthetic; nearest-neighbor over a 150-sample).
At every setting the mechanistic generator's DCR sits at or above the real-to-real baseline — it is
never closer to real records than real records are to one another — and verbatim copies are
essentially absent. Increasing *k* both anonymizes and, by densifying the alphabet, improves fidelity;
the vocabulary grows with the city (m=3 → 959 symbols, versus 71 on the small PoC), so fine
granularity remains sparse even at n=581, motivating the scaling study.

### 5.4 Data efficiency (RQ3)

Fixing the vocabulary (m=2, k=5) and varying the number of training individuals *n* isolates the
effect of scale (Table 2, 3 seeds). Fidelity improves monotonically — bigram-TV falls from 0.75 at
n=20 to 0.33 at n=581 — while DCR stays above the real-to-real baseline (0.27) at every *n* and copies
remain ≈0. The generator is thus data-efficient *and* structurally leak-free; the small-sample
limitation of the PoC is real, and scale addresses it.

**Table 2. Data-scaling at fixed vocabulary (m=2, k=5).**

| n individuals | bigram TV↓ | DCR synth↑ | DCR real base | copies of training |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 20  | 0.750 | 0.496 | 0.267 | 0.0 |
| 50  | 0.695 | 0.507 | 0.267 | 0.7 |
| 100 | 0.640 | 0.503 | 0.267 | 0.0 |
| 300 | 0.459 | 0.453 | 0.267 | 0.0 |
| 581 | 0.329 | 0.409 | 0.267 | 0.3 |

### 5.5 Mechanistic versus deep across scale (RQ3, decisive)

Training a small LSTM and the order-1 Markov on the *same* subsample across *n* (Table 3) gives the
sharpest result. The LSTM leads on fidelity at small and mid *n* — but buys it by **memorizing**: 14.3%
of its output at n=20 are near-copies of training individuals, collapsing toward zero as data grows.
The Markov never copies. Moreover the fidelity advantage narrows and **reverses at n=581**, where the
simple, interpretable, DP-amenable Markov (bigram-TV 0.401) beats the LSTM (0.441) with zero leakage.

**Table 3. Deep (LSTM) vs mechanistic (Markov) across n (m=2, k=5, 2 seeds).**

| n | Markov bigram TV↓ | LSTM bigram TV↓ | Markov near-copies % | LSTM near-copies % |
|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| 20  | 0.779 | 0.674 | 0.0 | 14.3 |
| 50  | 0.735 | 0.563 | 0.3 | 1.3 |
| 100 | 0.675 | 0.521 | 0.3 | 0.7 |
| 300 | 0.500 | 0.439 | 0.0 | 0.3 |
| 581 | 0.401 | 0.441 | 0.3 | 0.3 |

For privacy-preserving synthesis the implication is clear: at small *n* — the restricted-access regime
that motivates the work — the deep model's fidelity is an artifact of copying real people; at scale
the mechanistic model is at least as faithful and never leaks. The mechanistic generator therefore
dominates the fidelity/privacy trade-off across the entire data range studied.

### 5.6 Spatial realism (RQ4)

On the proof-of-concept geography, the decoupled instantiation preserved the coarse spatial spread but
inflated jump lengths (real median 0.28 km vs 1.01 km) and the radius of gyration (2.36 vs 3.28 km),
because a spatially-blind semantic sequence realized at each type's nearest instance cannot reproduce
micro-locality. The **spatially-coupled** generator recovers it: it matches the radius of gyration
(2.44 vs 2.36 km) and restores the short-jump mode (0.72 km), with zero exact path copies; the residual
jump-length gap against continuously-sampled real GPS is a sampling-granularity artifact. `[coupled
generator at Paris-581 scale: pending]`

### 5.7 Limitations and threats to validity

(i) The deep baseline is a single small, untuned LSTM; a larger or better-tuned model could improve
fidelity at large *n* — though this would not undo the small-*n* memorization, which is the privacy
point. (ii) Validation so far is one city; New York provides the cross-city robustness and larger-*n*
membership-inference check. (iii) Privacy is measured empirically (DCR, copy-rate, MUITAS); a formal
(ε,δ)-DP guarantee via privatized counts is specified but not yet evaluated. (iv) Utility is not yet
measured with a TSTR downstream task. (v) One real individual with multiple OSM accounts would appear
as multiple "individuals"; this rare confound is inherited from the source dataset.

---

## 6. Conclusion

We argued and gave first evidence that a location-centric semantic summarization, MAT-SUM, is a natural
substrate for privacy-preserving synthesis of Multiple-Aspect Trajectories. Generating over a
k-anonymized semantic vocabulary yields synthetic mobility that is semantically rich, controllably
private and data-efficient — a combination no single existing technique offers — and a simple
mechanistic generator dominates a deep one on the fidelity/privacy trade-off. The natural next steps
are cross-city validation (New York), formal differential privacy on the generator's counts, a TSTR
utility study, and a membership-inference audit at scale.
